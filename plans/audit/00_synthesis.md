# Locus feature audit — synthesis

Every navigable surface and significant subsystem in Locus — 48 of them, listed
in `feature-inventory.md` — was put through a five-round critique loop by five
agents defined in `.claude/agents/`: four independent domain critics (a UX
researcher, a church operations director, a youth ministry director, a
children's ministry director) and an ideation agent that synthesised each
round's critiques into a proposal the next round attacked.

Round 1 judged the code. Rounds 2–5 judged the proposal. The per-area reports
(`core-hygiene.md`, `gamification.md`, `pastoral-ops.md`,
`engagement-analytics.md`, `content-giving-comms.md`, `relational-tools.md`)
carry the per-feature verdicts and the work lists. This file is the part that
only shows up when you look at all six together.

---

## The headline

**Locus has been optimising for feature count, and the count was the problem.**
Of 48 surfaces and subsystems, the loop cut or dissolved 25, merged or demoted
9, and found real defects in most of what survived. No area proposed a net
addition. The one substantial *build* the audit recommends — grade promotion —
already exists as a button that does nothing.

Underneath that sit four patterns that recur across areas and explain most of
the individual findings.

### 1. Invented numbers presented as findings

The largest single category. Not demo shortcuts — features that look, read and
behave exactly like real reports:

* **Global Pulse** charted the church against "anonymized global averages". No
  benchmark backend exists; every global value was a literal and three of five
  local values carried the author's own `// Mocked` comment, plotted on the same
  axes in the same style as the two real ones.
* **Giving Trends** computed giving as `attendance * 25` through a sine wave
  ("to look natural"), branded **Stripe** — a vendor Locus has no integration
  with. **Giving River** drew six-figure fund flows from hardcoded constants.
  **Sermon Sentiment** carried the same `attendance * 25` series under an axis
  reading "Giving Volume ($)". Locus has no Giving API access at all.
* **Campus Cup** injected `Math.random()` opponent scores on a 3-second timer.

The church operations critic put the cost plainly: a pastor or finance chair
citing either giving number in a stewardship conversation would be repeating a
fabricated statistic they had every reason to trust.

### 2. Safety features that do not protect

Mechanisms presented as protection that do not provide it. Worse than their
absence, because it is the careful user who reaches for them.

* **Sandbox Mode fails open.** *(Corrected — see `CORRECTION-sandbox.md`. The
  audit originally reported this as inert, which was wrong: a service worker at
  `public/sandbox-sw.js` really does intercept the tagged writes. Every critic
  grepped `mock-api/` and missed it.)* Nothing checked that the interceptor was
  in control before attaching the header and sending the write — so on a first
  load, or after a failed registration, the PATCH reached Planning Center for
  real while the banner still read "changes are simulated".
* **Emergency Alerts' Twilio send** was a `setTimeout` that always reported
  success. During an actual emergency a pastor would believe a message went out.
* **Encryption without a secret.** `storage.ts` passes `appId` — the PCO
  Application ID, not a secret — as the PBKDF2 passphrase, and falls back to
  plaintext `JSON.parse` when decryption fails.

### 3. Fields that exist only in the fixture

`prayer_topic`, `first_time_giver`, `first_gift_date`, `anniversary` and
`background_check_expires_at` are generated by the same `Math.random()` block in
`mock-api/data.js` and read straight off the flat People response. None is a
real PCO People attribute. Every feature built on them — the Co-Pilot's
"spiritual climate", Sentiment Pulse, the First Time Giver lane, the Expired
Background Checks lane — is fully functional in the demo and permanently empty
in production.

The inverse is the sharpest finding of the lot: `passed_background_check`, the
one clearance field PCO *does* expose, has **zero occurrences** anywhere in the
repository. It was never fetched.

### 4. The tests encode the defects

The 93 test files are evidence of stability, not correctness. `newsletter.test.ts`
was built entirely from `isChild: true` fixtures and asserted by name that
minors' birthdays *must* appear in the newsletter — the leak was the specified
behaviour. `family.test.ts` asserts on a fixture where a ten-year-old is marked
"Dad" and passes silently. `App`-level tests mock `storage.ts` wholesale, which
is why a passphrase bug that silently wiped every user's progress survived. Any
"keep the tests green" pass would have re-introduced all three.

## The structural conclusion

All four critics, independently and unanimously in round 4:

> **Locus Intelligence no longer justifies being a separate workspace.**

It is not a judgement about the executive dashboard's ambitions. It is what
remains once the cuts land: Area D's analytics go from eight nav rows to zero,
Area E dissolves, Prayer Match and Locus Public are deleted — and Intelligence
becomes a second sidebar, a second layout, a second auth overlay and a
pre-credential workspace picker wrapped around a permission-scoped filter of
Core. It should fold into Core as a role-gated view.

Two things must be said alongside it, because the audit found them the hard way:

* **The role split was never a security boundary.** It is a client-side `if`.
  Locus Public — a member-record editor with a name-picker and no member
  authentication — was reachable *only* from the surface documented as
  read-only, and its Save called the same PCO write path as everything in Core.
* **Per-user OAuth is an explicit non-goal.** Until it exists, the folded view is
  UI convenience and must never be described as viewer-safe.

## What was implemented in this session

Everything below is committed, with the full suite green.

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| Newsletter birthday block is adults-only via a shared `isMinor` predicate; test suite rewritten | It published every minor's name and birthday, one click from "Copy Markdown", and the tests required it to |
| `isMinor` adopted by the small group sorter and recruitment, and unit-tested | Same staleness, same exposure, in two more places |
| Small group sorter refuses a roster containing minors instead of silently filtering | A tool that quietly thins its input teaches you to trust the wrong output |
| `saveGamificationState` passes `appId` at all six call sites | Three passed `auth`, so state written by those paths could never be read back and silently reset to zero |
| Member names no longer sent to `ui-avatars.com` | Seven call sites across six screens leaked real names, children included, to a third party on render |
| Schenectady zips no longer resolve to area code 555 | The reserved fictional range was being written into live phone numbers |
| Newcomer funnel counts Guest check-ins | It dropped the population it exists to count, and mis-dated long-time attenders as newcomers |
| Partial bulk-write failure reports honestly and keeps Undo available | It told the operator everything was reverted while the written half sat in PCO |
| Component tests mock only the network, not whole modules | A stubbed module let the minor guards pass vacuously |
| Sandbox Mode fails closed | It attached the header and sent the write without checking the interceptor was in control; now it refuses to send, and verifies the reply came from the sandbox |
| Scoring substrate deleted rather than gated; `GamificationState` 13 fields → 1 | No predicate over (before, after) can tell a verified correction from an accepted guess, and the one-click bulk fixer proved it |
| **Deleted (24 surfaces):** Global Pulse, Giving River, Giving Trends, Sermon Sentiment, Sermon Correlator, Emergency Alerts, Volunteer Web, Robert Report, Genealogy Graph, Pastoral Co-Pilot, Predictive Attrition, Check-in Velocity, Solar System, Heatmap of Life, Sentiment Pulse, Prayer Partner Match, Locus Public, Integrations Hub, Map View's route, Golden Record, Bounty Board, Campus Cup, Achievement Case, Avatar + confetti + badges | Fabricated data, fake sends, fields PCO does not have, or dead code — all unanimous across five rounds |

Net effect so far: **−11,004 / +523 lines** across 134 files.

## What is specified but not built

The per-area reports carry ordered work lists with file paths. The largest items:

1. **Ghost Protocol repair** — it PATCHes real records to `inactive` in a loop
   that bypasses the undo stack, with no confirm, no per-record deselect, a list
   truncated to 10 while archiving all, criteria text that no longer matches the
   logic, and never-checked-in newcomers flagged as ghosts.
2. **Grade promotion** — the highest-value thing the product could get right,
   currently a button that fires `alert('...Mocked action')` and hides the row.
3. **The Intelligence fold-in**, with the write-path guard as its precondition.
4. **The remaining subtraction** — roughly fifteen more surfaces.

## What could not be settled

* Whether a volunteer motivation layer can exist at all without per-person
  identity. The loop concluded the score should be deleted rather than gated,
  because `Smart Fix All` makes any correctness gate computable by a script —
  but nobody could say what a volunteer can honestly be congratulated for.
* Small-group-specific student drift, the signal the youth director says
  actually matters, is not buildable from People + Check-Ins alone.
* Whether the PCO token can write notes, which the durable archive ledger
  depends on. Answerable only against a live token.
